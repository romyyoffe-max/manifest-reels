Brain feeling foggy? Sprout has a 40-second fix 🐢🌱

At the University of Melbourne, 150 students did a dull attention test: press a key for every digit, except 3. Halfway through, they got a 40-second break looking at a photo of a city rooftop. The only difference: half saw a bare concrete roof, half saw the same roof covered in a flowering meadow. Back on the test, the concrete group's attention kept slipping, while the green group missed about half as many targets in the second half (0.68 vs 1.43 missed on average) and responded more steadily (Lee et al., 2015).

Why? Scientists think focused attention tires with use, and nature catches your eye gently, without effort, so your focus gets a moment to recover (attention restoration theory, Kaplan, 1995). Honest note: it's one study with small differences, so think "a tiny reset", not a superpower.

Try this today:
1. When you feel foggy, find something green (a tree, a plant, even a photo)
2. Look at it for 40 seconds
3. Then get back to your dream
